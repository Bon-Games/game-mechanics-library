Harvesting transfers a source's available output into usable resources through an interaction, tool, or machine. Picking a ripe crop or cutting grain collects its yield and changes the source's state; growing the crop and selling or processing the result are separate steps.[1][10] Whether a source disappears, needs replanting, or regenerates is a game-specific rule, not the definition itself.

## Where it is used

Farming simulations and farm-focused life RPGs use harvesting as a recurring link between cultivation, income, and production.[1][7][8] Implementations can extend collection to trees, flowers, and animal products.[9] It also suits survival/crafting sandboxes and strategy economies; the listed examples focus on farming rather than every possible gathering system.

## Example

A farmer cuts a strip of ripe wheat with a combine. The standing crop becomes a harvested strip while grain fills the machine's tank. When the tank is full, the farmer unloads into a trailer before continuing; the grain can later be sold, stored, or processed.[1]

## Why developers use it

- **Design:** Make preparation pay off through a tangible collection step, with choices about timing, routes, tools, and how to use the yield.
- **Development:** Keep readiness, yield, source depletion, and storage transfer consistent; handle full inventories and simultaneous collection without lost or duplicated rewards.
- **Production:** Reuse a collection framework across resource types, but budget for distinct growth states, tool animations, feedback, and save/load QA. Repetition can become busywork without pacing or efficiency upgrades.

## What the player sees and feels

Ready crops, a picking or cutting action, an emptied patch, and an item pickup or rising storage count make the resource transfer readable.[1][10] A cleared field can feel like earned progress and relief after waiting; unclear readiness or repetitive inputs can instead feel like chores. These design, production, and experience observations are editorial synthesis, not guaranteed outcomes.
